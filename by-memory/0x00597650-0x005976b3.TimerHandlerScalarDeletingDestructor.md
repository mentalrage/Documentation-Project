## Behavior

The wrapper reinstalls the `TimerHandler` vtable on the object, removes pending timer events for the handler through [UID:0000SI][g_pTimerMgr](by-global/g_pTimerMgr.md) and the owner-removal helper at `0x00597a10`, then conditionally frees the object if the deleting-destructor flag has bit `0` set.

This is generated destructor glue, not a handwritten public timer helper. Its source-level representation should come from the `TimerHandler` destructor declaration and compiler-generated deleting-destructor machinery.

## First-Draft C++ Decision

Do not emit a handwritten scalar deleting destructor body. The wrapper is compiler-generated from the virtual destructor/deleting-destructor ABI. Emitting a normal source helper would be artificial and could duplicate cleanup already represented by [UID:0001K7][0x00597580-0x005975c3.TimerHandlerDestructor](by-memory/0x00597580-0x005975c3.TimerHandlerDestructor.md) and the class declaration.

Target-specific no-code proof:

1. The only proven incoming route is the vtable deleting-destructor slot.
2. The body is the standard vtable restore, non-deleting destructor semantics, and optional delete wrapper.
3. The human source should declare/define the destructor, not manually author a scalar deleting-destructor helper.
4. The non-deleting destructor page is the correct source-level cleanup body.
